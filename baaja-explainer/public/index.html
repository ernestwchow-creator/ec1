    How we rate

    Ground rules

    • The base text is the Senate-passed substitute to H.R. 2347. Each verdict applies to that text.
    • A claim about what the bill says is checked against the bill text itself, quoted with page and line numbers. Where we haven't quoted the text, we rely on the Bipartisan Policy Center's section-by-section. A claim about what the bill will do is checked against independent modeling, and the verdict says how certain that modeling is.
    • Claims are paraphrased unless the exact words matter. The speaker is named so you can find the original.
    • Claims from both sides get the same scale and the same checks. A verdict rates one factual claim. It is not a verdict on the bill.
    • Characterizations ("guts," "abandons," "skyrocketing," "truly bipartisan") are rated on their factual core, and marked down when the wording overstates it: one step for overstatement, more when the wording isn't supported by the evidence.
    • Supporters' and opponents' material appears in a random order on each visit, and both sides share the same neutral colors.
    • Every claim lists the sections it depends on. If the House changes one of those sections, the claim is flagged for review.
